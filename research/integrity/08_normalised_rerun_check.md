## ERRORS

1. experiments.tex:101 claimed "74.0" (WFQ utility, truthful hotspot). Correct: 73.9 (compare.json mean 73.948; tables/compare.tex prints 73.9). Severity: error (rounding, trivial).
2. experiments.tex:192 claimed "PAYG devices gain a median of 2.4%". Correct: 2.35 (deviation.json repay_tier_summary PAYG median_rel_gain_pct 2.3458, recomputed from records), i.e. 2.3%. Severity: error (trivial).
3. experiments.tex:585 claimed "the complete allocation step ... takes 2.2 ms per slot at 75 devices". Current results/compare.json: REPAY hotspot ms_per_slot mean 1.48 (median 1.48, max 1.89 over 10 seeds); mix.json repay at ~75 devices similar. The 2.19 quoted in research/integrity/05_final_check.md came from the pre-regeneration compare.json. Wall-clock, machine-dependent, but the stated figure no longer matches the shipped result file. Suggest "about 1.5 ms". Conclusion (>2 orders of magnitude headroom) still holds. Severity: error (stale number).
4. experiments.tex:405 "When one device in ten is real-time, serving all of them first costs the other nine less on average than in the synthetic hotspot". Not supported in relative terms. Elastic-rate cost of priority relative to REPAY: synthetic 1.26 vs 1.74 = -28%; Paris 0.32 vs 0.44 = -28% (WFQ -27%/-28%; plain DPP synthetic -13%, Paris -11%). It is smaller only in absolute Mbit/s because elastic demand is light on the traces. Also in tension with the abstract's "elastic rate 11% to 28% lower" and with experiments.tex:404. Severity: error (explanation contradicted by the data).
5. discussion.tex:30 "the same cap and the plain priority rules all serve many more RT slots than REPAY at little average cost to elastic devices". True only for capped priority (elastic rate -0.007 +- 0.014). WFQ and Priority cost 27-28% of the elastic rate (0.12 Mbit/s), plain DPP 11% (real_compare paired: -0.118, -0.123, -0.046). research/integrity/07 item 4 had already flagged this. Severity: error.

## IMPRECISE

6. intro.tex:36 "in our simulations between half and two thirds of the devices still gain a small amount by misreporting". Data: 62.8% (180 devices), 58.3% (60 devices), 45% (rho_RT=10%), 47.5% (Paris traces), 50.8% (Grand Paris Sud), 23.3% (Mesa). The range is 23%-63%; "between half and two thirds" holds only for the synthetic runs.
7. abstract.tex (current, line 8) "On public Wi-Fi traces, where real-time sessions are rare, REPAY gains 7 points over PF but priority rules serve 12 to 15 points more, at a class envy 58 to 122 times larger". These are Paris PW0127 numbers only (REPAY-PF 7.0; DPP/WFQ/Priority minus REPAY 13.8/11.7/14.7; envy ratios 58/116/122). Grand Paris Sud: REPAY-PF 4.8, priority-type gap 7.5-8.4 points, envy ratio 91-161. Mesa: REPAY-PF 14.2, gap 3.1-6.3 points, ratio 10-18. The same generalisation is in discussion.tex:40 (conclusion: "when real-time traffic is rare plain priority serves 12 to 15 points more of it"). In the earlier 683-word abstract the wording said "In a trace-driven scenario", which was correct.
8. abstract.tex (current, line 8) "some devices in thin tiers exceed the incentive bound". Of 9 exceedances on Paris, 8 are free-tier devices (free is 88% of devices, not thin) and 1 is a thin premium tier; on Grand Paris Sud all 5 are free tier. Only the factor-of-twelve device is a thin-tier case. (experiments.tex:450-452 states this correctly.)
9. abstract.tex (current, line 7) "against 77% to 170% under other class-aware rules" omits capped priority, a class-aware rule that at q=0.3 has median -20% (frontier.json, 60 devices) and 113% only at rho_RT=10%. Values 77/154/170 are correct for plain DPP/WFQ/Priority (deviation.json). Also "a misreport gains at most the bound per switch" paraphrases the static and dynamic theorems loosely.
10. experiments.tex:521 JS "serves ... 90.3% of RT slots on the traces, at or below its delivery target of 0.9". 90.3% (real_compare/baselines part 3) is above 0.9; on hotspot 85.5% is below. (Also RT success counts slots with rate >= rmin while the target is a delivery ratio; same concept in the adaptation, but "at or below" is wrong for the traces.)
11. experiments.tex:31-32 "with three to ten seeds these intervals are indicative": experiments with two seeds/traces exist (reverse deviation, oracle ablation, capped-priority deviations, additional-baseline deviations, low-RT-share deviations) and the trace-driven ones use six. Counts in line 32 themselves all match the scripts (see ok list). Run lengths of optgap (20000), tiers series (12000) and binding table (8000) and real (7200) are not stated in the Setup.
12. experiments.tex:102 "REPAY-E differs by at most 0.01 in utility and 0.1 point in video success". Mean differences: 0.011 and 0.05; per-run maxima 0.024 and 0.10. Fine as a bound on means for video, borderline for utility.
13. experiments.tex:52 caption "gaming bounds are 100, 200 and 150 Mbit": exact values 100.2, 200.2, 150.2 (Params.B). Rounded; ok-but-note.
