## (4) Consistency with the rest of the paper
- Intro "what the approach does not do": says incentive guarantee approximate and "about half of the devices still gain a small amount". Trace-driven results add that 10 of 120 exceed the bound, one by about tenfold; add a clause. It also mentions only the well-tuned cap serving more RT slots; in the trace-driven scenario even DPP/WFQ/Priority (12-15 points) and the untuned cap (9 points) do, at higher envy; and REPAY at V=20 gives up 12-15 points. Conclusion has no mention of this trace-driven loss ("capped strict priority can serve more real-time slots at a higher elastic rate" is the only statement; in the trace-driven scenario the cap has the same, not higher, elastic rate).
- Discussion: "the variant without the debt price does as well as REPAY on envy" holds for main (43 vs 42) but not under the strict rule (45 vs 16); and the sentence "the debt price mainly lowers envy, from 28 to 19 Mbit" is a synthetic-scenario statement that the trace-driven main results contradict; scope it.
- Discussion lists the limitations (heuristic classes, rough tiers, unrelated sources) consistently with the section. "several experiments use two to five traces" does not cover the six here; harmless.
- The section's claim "the pattern is the same ... under the stricter rule" is fine; it states the 12-15 point cost openly, consistent with abstract.
