# R3: Handling-editor assessment and devil's advocate

Manuscript: "Priority Now, Payback Later: Online Capacity Allocation with Bounded Class Envy for Tiered Internet Access" (REPAY). 30 pages, about 12,000 words, 9 sections. Read: abstract, intro, related, model, algorithm, analysis (statements and key proofs), experiments, discussion, appendix (skimmed), and lit note 05 (closest prior art).

---

## Part A. Editor assessment

### A1. Summary of the contribution
The paper proposes REPAY, a drift-plus-penalty allocator for one bottleneck link. Real-time (RT) flows get priority in a shortage. Each elastic device accumulates a per-device deficit queue that records what it would have received had it claimed the RT bundle. The deficit raises the device's own priority and the tier's price of RT priority. Results:
1. A deterministic sample-path bound on every deficit, `D <= V w eta + r_max` (Thm 1), which gives "window class-envy-freeness".
2. A price-taking incentive corollary (static misreport gains at most B; switching gains at most B per switch).
3. An O(1/V) utility gap against a benchmark of stationary, envy-free-in-expectation policies (Thm 4).
4. Synthetic simulations: 90% vs 82% RT success against PF; the median false-video device gains +170% under Priority, +154% under WFQ, +62% under plain DPP and +0.2% under REPAY.

### A2. Positioning, honesty and coherence
Strengths:
- The paper is unusually candid. It says outright that the credit idea is not new (Karma, BoPF, Credit Fairness, karma economies, Wong et al.) and that the theorem technique (deterministic virtual-queue bounds) is borrowed. The limitations section is substantive: large-market and price-taking caveats, uplink demand, bound looseness, synthetic traces, a Karma baseline that is "our adaptation".
- The Theorem 1 proof is short and checkable. It is a neat cancellation argument that holds for arbitrary prices, arrivals and capacities.
- Negative results are reported in the paper itself: the incumbents-first rounding bug, Proposition 5 (window envy does not control slot-by-slot re-declaration), and the oracle-attack check.

Weaknesses in framing and coherence:
- **The headline claim is mis-ranked.** The title and abstract lead with "bounded class envy" and the intro with "telling the truth becomes the best policy". The proved incentive result is conditional on a price-taking hypothesis that the paper itself calls "a hypothesis, not a consequence of the model". Abstract wording such as "a static misreport gains at most this constant" is accurate only with "for price-taking devices" attached. It is attached, but in a subordinate clause that most readers will drop. The incentive claim should be framed as "bounded envy plus an approximate incentive consequence under price-taking, validated empirically".
- **The abstract's final sentence overstates what the simulation shows.** "No device gains more than a third of the bound" is a statement about a synthetic six-trace sample with a video cap of 3 Mbit/s. The discussion admits that this cap "by itself makes the deviation unattractive to devices with a large fair share". That caveat belongs next to the number.
- **Two results are in tension and the paper does not reconcile them in the headline.** The abstract says misreporting is deterred, yet Section 6 reports that 51% of devices gain something from a false video declaration under REPAY (small gains, but a majority). The abstract reports only the median change (+0.2%) and the max-over-bound ratio, which hides the fact that about half the population has a positive incentive to deviate. See Attack 6.
- **Bound magnitude is relegated.** At the default V=20 the video bound is 43 Mbit, and 83 Mbit or more for premium tiers. The gaming bound is larger because eta_g = 5. Against a median stay of 240 s at about 1.5 Mbit/s (roughly 360 Mbit), that is about 12% to 25% of a typical stay. For the 5-slot minimum stay the bound is vacuous. The theory-vs-practice gap for Theorem 4 (B_0 "far larger" than observed) is admitted only in the discussion and one parenthesis.
- **The paper hangs together structurally.** The order model, algorithm, analysis, experiments, discussion is clean. However, the analysis section (2,800 words) plus experiments (3,200 words) dominate. The perspective-reformulation, affine-majorant and rotation-rounding machinery is a lot for a reader to absorb before the main result.
- **Length.** About 30 pages with 12,000 words of main text is long for the incremental claim. The experiments contain many small sub-experiments (thin markets, collapse curves, oracle attack, variants, runtime) with 2 to 6 seeds each. It reads as a thorough technical report rather than a journal paper with one clear message.

### A3. Significance and originality
- Originality is genuine but narrow. Per-device deficit queues with a deterministic sojourn-wise bound, in a DPP framework with churn, combined with envy-freeness across heterogeneous service classes, did not turn up in the literature search (note 05, honest about residual risk: Hou-Borkar-Kumar 2009, Li-Wu-Li 2015 and Karsten et al. 2005 were not read in full). The novelty claim in the related-work section, "no result with guarantees per agent over a finite sojourn", is correctly hedged.
- Significance is moderate. The setting (a single access point, slot-level rates, three classes, three tiers) is a stylised model. The deployment value depends on a premise (class is declared and unverifiable) that is contestable (Attack 1).
- The theoretical core is thin relative to the machinery. Theorem 1 is a one-page cancellation argument; Theorem 4 is a standard DPP result with a non-informative constant; Theorems 2 and 3 are corollaries of Theorem 1 under price-taking.

### A4. Venue fit
- **Best fit:** *IEEE/ACM Transactions on Networking* or *IEEE Transactions on Network and Service Management*, or *Performance Evaluation* / *Queueing Systems* if the stochastic-network core is emphasised. The framing as access-network allocation with tiers is native to the networking venues.
- **Possible:** *Operations Research Letters* or *Naval Research Logistics* in a trimmed form that foregrounds Theorem 1 and the DPP/envy link. A top OR journal (*Operations Research*, *Management Science*, *MSOM*) would be a stretch: the economics (price-taking, no equilibrium analysis, no welfare theorem) is too thin. A top networking journal (ToN) would demand real traces or a testbed.
- **Not a fit as is:** a mechanism-design venue (EC, GEB), because the incentive result is not equilibrium-grounded.

### A5. Recommendation: **MAJOR REVISION**
The paper is honest, technically careful and publishable in a good specialist venue, but not in its current framing and evidentiary state. The principal gaps are (i) the premise is not defended against DPI/ML classification and QUIC, (ii) the incentive result rests on a price-taking assumption that the experiments only partly validate, (iii) the evidence is synthetic only, and (iv) the contribution over Karma/BoPF-type credit schemes is stated but not isolated empirically or theoretically.

### A6. The 5 most important required changes
1. **Defend the premise with evidence and a scoping statement.** Add a subsection that states exactly when the class is "declared and unverifiable" (DSCP/WMM access-category marking in Wi-Fi, 802.11 QoS maps, application-layer hints, uplink). Explain why DPI/ML classification is not a substitute (cost, error and adversarial evasion, regulation such as net neutrality rules, encryption including QUIC). Quantify what happens if classification is noisy: extend the model with a misclassification probability, and show REPAY degrades gracefully or composes with a classifier (the classifier gives the reference; the deficit handles residual error).
2. **Re-frame the incentive claim and reconcile the 51% figure.** Lead with "bounded envy; approximate incentives under price-taking; empirical evidence in a stated range" in title, abstract and intro. Report in the abstract the fraction of devices that gain and the aggregate gain, and give a single-sentence explanation of why a positive incentive for half the devices is consistent with a bound B (the bound is an upper limit, not a zero-gain claim). Add a "gain relative to the cost of lying" or an equilibrium/population-dynamics statement (Section 6 "when many misreport" is a start) as the main incentive evidence.
3. **Make the bound practically meaningful or say plainly it is not.** Provide (a) a table of B in Mbit and as a fraction of typical stay volume for each tier, class and V, (b) the per-slot incentive B/L for the stay-length distribution actually simulated (including the fraction of devices whose B/L exceeds some threshold), and (c) an operational recipe for choosing V (and gamma) to hit a target envy bound. Either tighten the constant (Remark "sharper constant" is a start; give an empirical tightness plot) or concede that the guarantee is informative only for stays much longer than B / (rate).
4. **Strengthen the empirical base beyond synthetic traces.** Use at least one real or publicly derived workload (for example a CRAWDAD or other Wi-Fi association trace for arrivals and sojourns; public video-call and download rate traces) and a packet-level or ns-3 / mac80211 check that slot-level rate allocation can be enforced (HTB or airtime weights). Increase seeds for the strategic experiments (2 seeds for the oracle and switching attacks, 3 for gaps, 6 for deviations are low) and state the number of devices and CI on every headline number.
5. **Isolate the contribution against closest prior art, quantitatively.** The "Credit" baseline is a class-blind Karma adaptation. Add (a) a class-aware credit baseline (Karma or BoPF-style with an RT bundle reference) and (b) an ablation separating the contributions: per-device deficit alone, deficit price feedback to the tier, tier queues, rotation. State in a short table what each of Karma, BoPF, Credit Fairness, DPP and REPAY guarantees (churn, classes, tiers, sample-path, incentives). Then trim the paper by 15% to 20% (move thin-market, variants, runtime and reverse-deviation material to the appendix).

Additional (lesser) requests: hedge the abstract sentence "no device gains more than a third of the bound" with its conditions; define "window class-envy-freeness" as a primary concept in the model with a justification for volume-based rather than utility-based envy; justify the 4x WFQ weight; state that the business-constraint slack in the default runs means the tier queues are mostly inactive in the headline comparison.

## Part B. Devil's advocate

Severity scale: Critical (could sink the paper or its central thesis), High (reviewers will press hard), Medium (weakens a claim), Low (presentational).

### Attack 1. The premise is shaky: operators can classify traffic, and where they cannot, the class need not be reported at all
**Severity: High to Critical.**
Argument: The whole construction exists because "the class of a flow is in effect declared by the device". But: (a) an access point already sees packet sizes, inter-arrival times and flow rates; ML-based traffic classification of encrypted flows (including QUIC and WebRTC) is mature, and real-time flows have a distinctive signature (small packets at constant cadence, bidirectional). (b) The users who can mislabel are those who control DSCP/WMM markings; most commodity Wi-Fi networks already rewrite or ignore DSCP at the edge. (c) Where real-time protection matters (RTC), the dominant solution is end-host congestion control and AQM (fq_codel, CAKE, L4S), which needs no class declaration at all and gives low latency to thin flows by design (sparse-flow optimisation). So one can argue that "priority for declared RT" is a strawman and that flow-queueing already protects real-time traffic without any declaration.
Where it hurts: the baselines are Priority, WFQ, PF and plain DPP, all class-declared or class-blind; none is a flow-queueing or classifier baseline. The 90% vs 82% RT success is against PF only.
Answer: (i) add an fq_codel/CAKE-style or classifier-based baseline (a classifier with accuracy p and an adversary that perturbs traffic shape); (ii) argue where flow queueing is not enough: RT flows needing a minimum rate (video at 0.5 to 3 Mbit/s) in shortage cannot be protected by fairness, and tier commitments require explicit control; (iii) show that REPAY composes with classification (the deficit as a safety net for classifier error and evasion); (iv) scope to markings the operator cannot or may not inspect (regulation, privacy, encrypted VPN tunnels, uplink).

### Attack 2. "Window class-envy-freeness" is a bespoke notion, and a constant bound that scales with V is the cost of the algorithm, not a fairness guarantee
**Severity: High.**
Argument: Class envy is defined as the volume difference to the RT flow's offer over any window. It compares an elastic device to a reference that is a fluid *offer* (not an allocation anyone actually gets in a rationed state), measures it in volume, and bounds it by `V w eta + r_max`. Two problems. First, the bound is a *design parameter*: V is chosen by the operator, and the same V sets the utility gap. A bound linear in V is not a fairness property in the usual sense (compare EF1, envy-freeness up to one good, which has an intrinsic, parameter-free meaning); it is a tunable tolerance. Second, volume is the right currency only if elastic users are indifferent to timing, which the paper asserts ("being repaid later costs them nothing") but elastic users with deadlines, interactive web browsing, or short stays do not satisfy. Under a short stay, B/L is large; the guarantee "weakly protects short visits" which are exactly the churn-heavy regime the paper motivates (trains, airports).
Also: the notion is one-directional (elastic envy of RT) and the reverse deviation is handled by a separate proposition with assumptions on utilities that "hold for the utilities of the model", i.e. by construction.
Answer: justify the notion axiomatically (relate to envy-freeness up to a constant in the fair-division literature, to Sinclair et al. and Benade et al.), show the lower bound (no rule can do better than order X without sacrificing utility, so B is not an artefact), report envy in utility terms for deadline-sensitive elastic traffic, and treat the stay-length distribution explicitly in the bound statement.

### Attack 3. The price-taking assumption is fatal for the thing the paper wants to claim
**Severity: Critical for the incentive claim; moderate for the rest.**
Argument: Theorems 2 and 3 hold only if the deviating device takes the multipliers and offers as those of the truthful path. But in a hotspot, the deficit queues feed into the tier price and the offers; a device that misreports changes the number of RT flows and elastic flows in its tier, hence the offers. The paper itself found that with incumbents-first rounding a device in a 3-device market gains linearly in its stay (a failure of the theory that was repaired by a rotation rule chosen after seeing the failure), and that a congestion-triggered or oracle switching strategy exceeds the price-taking prediction (25 to 43 Mbit per device) by far in the "actual system" because of a mechanism ("a device that leaves the elastic class forfeits its deficits") that the theory does not model and whose contribution "we did not separate". So the observed robustness is partly from an unanalysed implementation detail, and the paper cannot say which of the guarantees would survive if that rule were removed or gamed (for example by a Sybil identity that re-arrives). Dai et al. (2025) show primal-dual allocation is manipulable through dual variables; the paper acknowledges this and "sets this channel aside".
Also, the large-market claim (Azevedo-Budish strategy-proofness in the large) is invoked, but no formal large-market theorem is proved: no statement of the form "gain from deviation is at most epsilon(n) -> 0 as the market grows" is given. The paper only says it is "of the large-market type".
Answer: (i) prove a large-market statement (the externality of one device on offers is O(1/n) under bounded demand) or replace the invocation by an honest "approximate under price-taking"; (ii) ablate the forfeit-on-leave rule and the rotation to show which carry the empirical robustness; (iii) analyse Sybil/re-arrival (a device that disconnects and reconnects as a new device resets its deficit, potentially gaining a fresh credit-free RT claim, and note the bound is per device, not per owner); (iv) provide a best-response or equilibrium analysis for a population of strategic devices, not only unilateral deviation.

### Attack 4. The bound is vacuous or uninformative at practical parameter values, and Theorem 4 is admitted to be uninformative
**Severity: High.**
Argument: At the paper's own V=20, the video bound is 43 Mbit (premium 83 Mbit; gaming over 100 Mbit since eta_g = 5), against a mean elastic rate of 1.5 Mbit/s and a median stay of 240 s. A device can be short-changed by 12% to 25% of its entire stay's volume, and a device staying the minimum 5 slots is not protected at all (B/L about 8 Mbit/s per slot, above any rate). The largest observed deficit is a fraction of the bound, so the bound is loose (the tightness plot shows the bound is not binding in practice), meaning the *proved* guarantee says little about the *delivered* one. The discussion concedes Theorem 4's constant is "too large for the bound to be informative at the parameter values we simulate". So, of the three headline theorems, one is vacuous in practice and one is loose; only the qualitative form is supported. Meanwhile, the O(1/V) gap is "consistent with" simulation but proven only for the fluid allocation, whereas the deployed allocation is rounded (integer admission by rotation).
Answer: give a tightness analysis (a worst-case instance achieving a constant fraction of B), or improve the constant (population-aware, using n-bar as in the sharper-constant remark); present B as a function of V and the stay-length distribution; show an operating point where B/L is below a stated threshold for a stated share of devices; extend Theorem 4 to the rounded allocation or state clearly it does not cover it.

### Attack 5. The algorithm re-derives known deficit/credit ideas
**Severity: Medium to High.**
Argument: Serving a priority class now and repaying the others later through a per-user counter is Deficit Round Robin, Karma, BoPF, Hou-Kumar debt-based scheduling, Jaramillo-Srikant deficit counters, and Credit Fairness. The "new" ingredient is that the counter is a virtual queue in a DPP objective. But DPP with virtual queues for fairness constraints is routine (Neely's own fairness work, Huang et al. 2020), and the deterministic bound is a standard consequence of the max-weight structure (the queue stops growing when the weight exceeds the maximum marginal utility per unit rate). The paper itself says "we do not claim the technique, only its use for a per-device fairness property" and "our contribution is narrower and complementary". A referee can reasonably say: this is Karma with a different currency, plus Lyapunov notation.
What is genuinely different (and should be pushed): two service classes with different utility shapes inside a tier (RT is inelastic with a minimum rate; elastic is concave), per-sojourn guarantees with churn without heuristic bootstrap, price feedback to RT flows of the same tier, and commercial long-run constraints coexisting with the per-device queues.
Answer: add a feature-comparison table; implement a class-aware Karma or BoPF variant as a baseline; demonstrate a concrete scenario (a result) where Karma-with-bootstrap fails and REPAY succeeds on churn (the heuristic average-credit bootstrap can be gamed by arrival timing; test it); possibly show an impossibility or lower bound for credit schemes that do not couple deficits to the tier price.

### Attack 6. Half the devices gaining under the mechanism contradicts the incentive claim
**Severity: High (presentation), Medium (substance).**
Argument: 51% of devices gain something from falsely declaring video under REPAY (71% of free-tier devices, with a median gain of 1.2% for free). A mechanism that is "approximately incentive compatible" in which a majority has a strictly positive gain is not incentive compatible in any useful sense: the truthful profile is stable only if the gain is below the cost of lying, and the paper does not model any cost. The defence, that gains are small (95th percentile 0.05 Mbit/s, maximum 12.7 Mbit) and bounded by 0.30 B, is a defence of *approximate* IC with epsilon equal to the measured maximum, which is a number from six traces. In a population where all free devices have a +1.2% incentive, and misreporting is costless (flip a flag), an epsilon-IC mechanism is exploited by anyone indifferent; and the static analysis does not say what the equilibrium looks like when everyone deviates simultaneously (the "when many misreport" experiment shows REPAY at rho = 0 is stable, but that experiment varies a fraction rho of a fixed set with 4 traces and a single deviation type).
Further: the +0.2% median change is dominated by the premium and PAYG devices that lose (median -12% and -3%), which averages away the free-tier incentive. Reporting the median over the pooled population obscures the tier-level picture. The deterrent for premium/PAYG is itself a consequence of their tier weight and RT admission pattern, not of the deficit mechanism.
Answer: report the fraction gaining and the distribution by tier in the abstract and intro; state the guarantee as epsilon-IC with epsilon and its relation to B explicitly; include a tolerance/cost model (misreporting has an effort or risk cost c: the mechanism is IC for c above B/L); analyse the population equilibrium with a distribution of deviation costs; explain for the free tier why deviating pays (fair share below the video minimum, so the declared flow gets admitted by rotation, receiving the offer while the deficit repays in volume) and whether that is a design flaw (admission rotation gives a lottery ticket to impostors).

### Attack 7. The experiments are synthetic only and prove little about the real world
**Severity: High.**
Argument: Every trace is generated by the authors: Poisson arrivals with batch arrivals, geometric stays, two-state Markov demand, three-state Markov capacity with lognormal noise. The result files and seeds are public, which is good, but the generative model is chosen by the authors and parameters (V=20, weights 1/2/1.5, class probabilities 0.65/0.25/0.10, WFQ factor 4, video cap 3 Mbit/s, tolerance, hold-down tau) were "chosen before the comparison and not tuned", which cannot be verified and in any case concerns only the comparison runs. The seeds count is low in several places (2 for switching and oracle attacks, 3 for gaps and default business constraints, 4 for collapse curves). The strategic agents are scripted deviations (static video declaration, congestion-triggered, oracle) rather than learning agents; none of them includes a gaming class misreport or an elastic device that inflates demand. The model abstracts away the MAC layer: Wi-Fi capacity depends on the rate mix (airtime anomaly), so a slot-level rate allocation of 150 Mbit/s modulated by a Markov chain says nothing about whether the allocation is enforceable. Only the 10-seed comparison has a respectable sample. RT success is a slot-level threshold (rate at least r_min), not a QoE measure (latency, jitter, loss).
Answer: use public traces for arrivals and sojourn (campus Wi-Fi, CRAWDAD), real video-call rate traces, a Wi-Fi airtime model (or an ns-3 / hostapd + tc test on commodity hardware); report latency-aware RT metrics; use learning adversaries (e.g. no-regret or RL agents that best respond to the mechanism) to test the incentive claim; raise seeds to at least 10 everywhere and give paired CIs; add sensitivity to V, weights, class mix and stay length.

### Attack 8. Uplink, inflated demand and the measurement assumption
**Severity: Medium.**
Argument: The deficit is defined relative to the RT bundle and requires the access point to measure the elastic device's demand. For the downlink this is possible; for the uplink demand is inferred from buffer reports, which the discussion admits a device can inflate, and "we did not bound repeated accumulation". Since much real-time traffic (video calls, uploads) is symmetric or uplink-heavy, half of the motivating use case is outside the guarantee. Also, the model gives the device only one dimension of manipulation (class), whereas a real strategic device can also split flows, open parallel connections (a standard way to defeat per-flow fairness), spoof identities (Sybil), or time its arrivals.
Answer: add parallel-connection and Sybil analysis at least in the discussion with a bound (per-owner identification via MAC/authentication, or deficits tied to subscriber account); extend the model to inflated demand with a bound on accumulation; or restrict the claim in title and abstract to downlink.

### Attack 9. The utility theorem benchmark is partly self-serving
**Severity: Medium.**
Argument: Theorem 4 compares to "stationary policies that meet the business constraints in every population state and are envy-free in expectation". Because the benchmark is built to be the class of policies REPAY approximates, O(1/V) is nearly tautological for DPP; the interesting question, how much utility is lost relative to the *unconstrained* optimum (Priority/WFQ have higher utility when truthful: 74.8 and 73.9 vs 70.7), is answered only empirically and unflatteringly. The truthful-case utility loss of about 5% to 6% against Priority is the price of envy-freeness, and REPAY's advantage only appears once misreporting is accounted for. That is a legitimate trade, but then the paper's selling point is entirely the incentive claim, which is exactly the part with the weakest evidence (Attacks 3, 6, 7).
Answer: frame the result as a price-of-incentives analysis: utility loss vs envy bound vs incentive slack, as a Pareto frontier across V and gamma; state the lower-bound frontier if possible.

### Attack 10. Minor but visible issues
**Severity: Low to Medium.**
- The abstract states "serves 90% of RT slots against 82% for PF at the same delay-tolerant throughput", but Section 6 reports RT success of 89.9% (and the free tier 83% vs 70%); the abstract's "same throughput" corresponds to 1.75 vs 1.78 Mbit/s, which is a small difference with an unreported CI. Make sure the numbers are consistent and CIs are given.
- The bound is quoted for "every elastic device ... up to and including the slot after its last" but the incentive theorems require "sojourn L"; clarify boundary slots and the cost of the hold-down tau to genuine users who legitimately change class (a real user starting a call in the middle of a download is penalised by the hold-down).
- Remark and footnote content on an earlier implementation bug is informative but in a journal article belongs in the appendix.
- "Three commercial tiers with long-run business constraints" are slack in the headline comparison; the interesting binding case is a separate experiment with equal weights, so the combination of tiers and deficits is not tested in the main comparison.
- Keywords and title: "Tiered Internet Access" overstates scope; the model is one bottleneck link with slot-level rates.

## Part C. Consolidated prioritised revision roadmap

**Priority 1 (must do; decides publishability)**
1. Premise defence and classifier comparison (Attacks 1, 8). Add noisy-classifier model, an fq_codel/CAKE or classifier baseline, and a clear scope statement (declared or unverifiable classes; downlink; account-bound identities). Effort: moderate (new experiments plus about 1 page).
2. Reframe incentive claims and expose the 51% (Attacks 3, 6). Lead with bounded envy plus approximate incentives under price-taking; report fraction gaining and per-tier results in abstract and intro; add either a large-market lemma or an equilibrium/cost-of-lying analysis; ablate the forfeit-on-leave rule and rotation; address Sybil/re-arrival. Effort: high (one theorem or a careful analysis plus ablations).
3. Real or realistic evidence (Attack 7). Add at least one public-trace-driven scenario and an enforcement check (ns-3 or a tc/hostapd test); raise seeds; add learning adversaries; add latency-aware RT metric. Effort: high.

**Priority 2 (strongly recommended)**
4. Make the bound operational (Attack 4): table of B in Mbit and as a share of stay volume; B/L distribution; a recipe for V and gamma; a tightness example or sharper constant; state clearly that Theorem 4 is qualitative and does not cover rounding. Effort: low to moderate.
5. Prior-art isolation (Attack 5): feature table; class-aware credit baseline; ablation of deficit, price feedback and tier queues; a churn scenario in which heuristic bootstrap fails. Effort: moderate.
6. Justify the notion (Attack 2): axiomatic or lower-bound justification of window class envy; relate to EF-up-to-a-constant; report envy in utility terms for deadline-sensitive elastic traffic. Effort: moderate.

**Priority 3 (polish)**
7. Reframe Theorem 4 as a price-of-incentives frontier (Attack 9); add utility-envy-incentive trade-off plot.
8. Trim the paper by 15% to 20%: move thin markets, variants, runtime, reverse deviation, the early-implementation footnote and proof details to the appendix; a single clear message in the introduction.
9. Fix the abstract: hedge "no device gains more than a third of the bound" with the video cap and sample size; make numbers consistent with Section 6 and report CIs; qualify "Tiered Internet Access" or add "single bottleneck" to the title or abstract.
10. Add an explicit statement in the discussion about hold-down cost to legitimate class switchers and about the uplink limitation in the abstract.

**Venue plan:** after Priorities 1 and 2, submit to IEEE/ACM Transactions on Networking or IEEE TNSM (with real traces or a testbed). If only a theory-centred trimmed version is feasible, target Performance Evaluation or Operations Research Letters, foregrounding Theorem 1 and the DPP-envy link and dropping the incentive claims to a remark.
