# R1: Theory referee report (model, algorithm, analysis, appendix)

Manuscript: "Priority Now, Payback Later: Online Capacity Allocation with Bounded Class Envy for Tiered Internet Access" (REPAY).
Scope: paper/sections/{model,algorithm,analysis,appendix}.tex, cross-checked against repay/solver.py, repay/mechanisms.py, repay/params.py and experiments/check_credit_monotonicity.py. Related-work positioning was read from related.tex, intro.tex, abstract.tex and discussion.tex.

## 1. Summary of the submission

REPAY is a drift-plus-penalty (DPP) allocator for one bottleneck. There are three tier virtual queues Q_j (premium service level, PAYG service level, free floor). Each elastic device i carries one deficit queue D_{i,k} per real-time (RT) class k. D_{i,k} reflects the volume that i would have received had it declared k, minus what it got. The per-slot problem is made concave by a perspective reformulation of the RT term a*u_k(y/a) and an affine majorant of min(z,d). It is solved by bisection on the capacity multiplier. The results are as follows.

- **Theorem 1** (deficit bound): D_{i,k} <= V w_g eta_k + rmax_k, deterministically. It gives window class-envy-freeness (Corollary 1).
- **Theorems 2 and 3** (static and dynamic misreport): bounds on the volume gained by a price-taking misreporter, namely B for a static misreport and B per switch for the time spent posing as RT.
- **Proposition "reverse"**: a one-slot statement that RT flows do not pose as elastic.
- **Theorem 4** (labelled `thm:opt`): for the fluid allocation, utility is within B_0/V of a per-population-state benchmark, and constraint violation is O(sqrt(V/T)).

Overall I find the mathematics that is stated mostly correct. I verified Theorem 1, Remark "sharper constant", the drift algebra and a.s. argument of Theorem 4, the solver proposition, the majorant, Proposition "reverse" and Proposition "baselines" line by line (details in Section 4). The problems are of a different kind.

- The three guarantees are proved for three different objects: the rounded allocation, the fluid allocation, and a price-taking hypothetical deviator.
- The benchmark of Theorem 4 is so restrictive that it certifies little.
- The incentive claims rest on a hypothesis (price-taking, Def. 4) that absorbs the hard part of the problem.
- The "large-market" label is not justified by the form of the bound.
- The theoretical novelty is modest relative to the paper's own citations.

## 2. Recommendation: MAJOR REVISION

The central deterministic bound is correct and neat. The paper is candid about several limits. However, the headline claims in the title, abstract and conclusion ("best envy-free policy", "telling the truth becomes the best policy", "large-market type", "per device guarantees") exceed what Sections 4.2 and 4.3 prove. The algorithm that is simulated is not the algorithm that is analysed (rounding). The strongest incentive statement is conditional on an assumption that is essentially the conclusion. I would accept after the claims are brought in line with the proofs, and after either the rounded algorithm is analysed or the rounding interaction is stated honestly. The gap between the benchmark and a meaningful comparator should also be addressed.

## 3. Major concerns

### M1. Which allocation does each theorem concern? The simulated and the analysed algorithm differ, and a rounding bias breaks Theorem 4 for the real algorithm
Location: algorithm.tex "From fractions to flows"; analysis.tex Thm 1 proof last paragraph of its first part; Thm 4 preamble ("concerns the fluid allocation"); mechanisms.py lines computing `rate`, `rho`, `eps`, `self.Q`.

What is wrong:

(a) Theorem 1/Cor. 1 hold for the rounded algorithm. This is true and nicely argued: realised x >= fluid x, and D is updated with the fluid reference.

(b) Theorem 4 is proved only for the fluid allocation, but the experiments, the incentive discussion and the abstract's narrative concern the rounded one.

(c) In the code, Q is updated with the realised rate vector (`rho = bincount(tier, weights=rate)`), not the fluid one. Rounding moves capacity across tiers. The remainder (na-M)z of a marginal group is either given to one extra flow at a reduced rate or pooled to elastic devices of any tier, ranked by marginal value.
- The realised tier rate therefore differs from the fluid one by up to rmax_k per marginal group per slot. The error is biased against whichever tier's RT group is marginal.
- In the DPP drift this adds a term Q_j(t)(e_j^{real} - e_j^{fluid}). It is multiplicative in the queue, not an additive constant, so it cannot be absorbed into B_0.
- If the bias is persistent (e.g. premium RT group marginal for long stretches, constraint tight), a linear drift in Q_PS cannot be excluded. The feedback may or may not compensate. No statement is proved.

(d) The text claims "the fluid reference ... is at least the average rate of the admitted flows of the group". This is false for the leftover flow when d_i < z. A single leftover flow served at L_r = (na-M)z gives the deviator min(L_r, d) per slot. This can exceed (na-M)·min(z,d) (e.g. d = 1, z = 3, na-M = 0.5 gives min(1.5,1) = 1 > 0.5). The statement "rounding errs in favour of the elastic device" therefore needs an extra hypothesis (d >= z or d >= rmax).

What would fix it: either (i) prove a rounding lemma with explicit constants (|rho_g^{real} - rho_g^{fluid}| <= rmax per slot, no systematic sign if the marginal fraction is randomised rather than floored) and carry the additive Q-term into Thm 4 with a Slater-type argument, or (ii) randomise the rounding (admit floor(na) flows plus one more with probability frac(na), serving the leftover at rate z) so that E[realised] = fluid, and state Thm 4 for that randomised rule, or (iii) clearly mark Thm 4 as a statement about an idealised relaxation in the abstract, intro item 5, and discussion, and give an empirical measure of the rounding discrepancy (realised vs fluid e_j, long-run averages).

Also, code rounds M = floor(na): with n = 1 and na z < rmin, the group is never served although the fluid solution admits it with a > 0, so the fluid reference a·min(z,d) overstates what the RT flow received. This is conservative for envy but not for utility.

### M2. The Thm 4 benchmark is per-state and effectively time-shifting-free, so the "O(1/V) of the best envy-free policy" claim is not supported
Location: analysis.tex eq. (bench), Thm 4 and the text after it; conclusion.tex ("remaining within O(1/V) of the best envy-free policy"); abstract.

What is wrong:

(a) Pi(xi) imposes E_{F_xi}[a m - x] <= 0 and E[e_j] <= 0 for every population state xi, with the expectation only over the single-slot capacity draw C ~ F_xi. The state xi_t includes all demand caps d_{i,t} (continuous), so essentially every slot has its own state. The benchmark therefore performs no intertemporal smoothing.

(b) Counterexample: take F_xi a point mass (constant capacity, as in Prop. 7(c) and any home scenario with a stable link). Then Pi requires a m_d(z) <= x in every slot, i.e. class-envy with B = 0. The paper itself says such a rule "offers no protection at all". U*(xi) is then the utility of the unprotected rule. REPAY may beat it (so the guarantee is one-sided, as acknowledged), but then Thm 4 gives no certificate that REPAY's utility is near the optimum for policies with envy bounded by B. The statement in conclusion.tex ("within O(1/V) of the best envy-free policy") is therefore not what is proved. "Best envy-free policy" should be replaced by "best per-state, in-expectation, zero-slack envy-free policy". The paper's own sentence "policies in Pi may give RT flows priority when capacity is low provided they compensate when it is high" holds only if the capacity within the same state varies; that is a lottery over independent draws, not time shifting within a finite sojourn.

(c) The majorant makes the benchmark strictly stronger than class-envy-freeness whenever some demand cap lies in (rmin, rmax); with this tolerated, the benchmark can be arbitrarily more conservative than the real constraint.

(d) Nonemptiness of Pi(xi) is required in every reachable state. This fails in exactly the interesting overload states (committed demand not coverable in expectation in that state). The theorem is silent when it matters and the paper's main motivating regime (transient shortage) is where the benchmark is empty or degenerate.

(e) Because Thm 1 shows D <= B, the per-device queues are always bounded; a sharper analysis can use this. The natural benchmark is a finite-sojourn one: policies whose per-device cumulative envy over each sojourn is <= B (or <= epsilon*L), with long-run (not per-state) business constraints. Such a benchmark would justify the "time shifting" narrative. It would also expose the actual trade-off between V in the envy bound and V in the utility gap.

What would fix it: (i) state the benchmark honestly in abstract, intro and conclusion; (ii) preferably prove a comparison against a stationary policy class defined over an ergodic or renewal population process with long-run per-device envy (e.g. average envy per unit time <= 0 for stationary users, with Slater slack epsilon, which yields the standard O(1/V) utility and O(V) queue bounds); (iii) discuss the example in (b) and show what U* is on constant capacity.

### M3. Assumption (ii) of Theorem 4 is violated by the experimental model
Location: analysis.tex Assumption 1(ii); repay/sim.py `_capacity` (3-state Markov chain with stay probability C_STAY = 0.97, times lognormal noise).

Assumption (ii) says C_t is drawn from F_{xi_t} given xi_t and "the whole past", i.e. C_t is conditionally independent of past capacities given the population. The simulated capacity is a persistent Markov chain, so conditional on xi_t and the past the law of C_t depends on C_{t-1}. Unless the regime is made part of xi_t (which makes xi_t no longer exogenous and arbitrary, as Assumption (i) requires), Theorem 4 does not apply to any experiment, including Section "exp-opt" which "measures the actual gap". If the regime is added to xi_t, per-state benchmark feasibility is further restricted (M2). The theory also states that "no stationarity or mixing of the population is required" but the capacity assumption is the real restriction.

What would fix it: allow capacity to be adversarial/Markov-modulated using the standard "regime = part of the state" device and say so; or prove the result under an ergodic-regime assumption with T-step frame drift (T-slot Lyapunov drift of Neely); or label the experimental comparison as out-of-theory.

### M4. Incentive claims: the price-taking hypothesis absorbs the hard part; the bound is not "large-market" (it does not vanish with market size); and volume bounds do not give utility incentives for the stated preferences
Location: model.tex ("true preference is an arbitrary nondecreasing function of cumulative volume"); analysis.tex Def. 4, Thm 2, Thm 3, text after Thm 2; related.tex (consistent with ... large-market sense of Azevedo-Budish); intro contribution 4.

What is wrong:

(a) Definition 4(ii) assumes that a deviator in class k receives an expected usable volume of at most r_{i,k,t}. Given that, Theorem 2 is a two-line corollary of Cor. 1. The substantive question (how much one device changes the offers of its tier, including n_{g,k} -> n_{g,k}+1, the change in the marginal fraction a, and the reaction of lambda) is exactly the correction term R^dev - R^tr which the paper says it does "not bound analytically". For a marginal group, adding a flow changes na-M by O(1) and thus a by O(1/n); the per-slot offer change is O(z/n), so over L slots the correction is O(Lz/n). With L = 240, z = 3, n = 10 this is of order 72 Mbit versus B = 43 Mbit. The bound "B/L per slot" is therefore accurate only up to an additive O(z/n) per slot that does not vanish with L.

(b) In Azevedo-Budish (2019), strategy-proofness in the large means epsilon -> 0 as the market grows, under semi-anonymity, a fixed finite type space and envy-freeness defined on the mechanism's outcome as a function of reports. The bound B/L here does not depend on market size at all (only on sojourn length L and V). It is a "long-stay" approximate IC statement, not a large-market one. REPAY is also history-dependent, with per-device queues and not semi-anonymous in the AB sense, and the hypotheses of the AB theorem are not verified. The paper says it "follows the approach"; related.tex, the intro and the abstract should not call the result "large-market type" without a statement of this form: gain per slot <= B/L + O(z/n) with an explicit constant, for a family of markets of growing size.

(c) The model states the elastic user's preference as an arbitrary nondecreasing function of cumulative volume. Then "volume gain at most B" gives no utility guarantee at all: take a threshold preference (needs S Mbit before leaving). If truth yields S - B/2 and the deviation yields S, the gain is the entire value of the file. The paper notes "for a preference that is Lipschitz in volume the utility gain is bounded accordingly", but this Lipschitz hypothesis is not stated as an assumption of Thm 2/3, and it contradicts the model sentence. Finite-file preferences also contradict Assumption 1(i) (exogenous demand caps). So approximate IC holds only for linear (or Lipschitz) preferences with persistent backlog.

(d) B/L is vacuous for short sojourns. With B = 43 Mbit and rmax = 3 Mbit/s, B/L > rmax for L < 15 slots; the hotspot trace has min_sojourn = 5. Dynamic theorem: the per-slot advantage while posing as RT is B/tau; with tau of tens of seconds this is of order rmax itself (e.g. tau = 20 gives 2.2 Mbit/s of 3). The theorem provides no useful bound for hold-down times short enough to be operationally acceptable.

(e) The dynamic theorem bounds only the advantage collected during the RT phases; the manuscript correctly states that it does not bound the total gain, and that credit monotonicity fails (the counterexample script exists). Hence there is no analytical incentive result for any strategy that mixes classes. Proposition 6 (`prop:commit`) shows the window technique cannot give one, but it concerns a frozen path and says nothing about REPAY (deficit forfeiture was the mechanism's own defence). The reader is left with: static misreport (conditional on price-taking) plus experiments. The abstract's formulation ("a switching strategy gains at most this constant per switch while it poses as real-time") is accurate but reads as an IC guarantee; it should say the guarantee does not cover total gain.

(f) The statement "being repaid later costs them nothing" (intro) is wrong for a finite sojourn. Deficits are discarded at departure, and Theorem 1 only guarantees volume >= reference - B. A device that leaves with a deficit near B has lost B Mbit relative to the offer of the RT class. B is the worst-case unrepaid debt, not zero.

What would fix it: state Thm 2/3 with an explicit second term and prove at least a crude bound on it in a symmetric large-group limit (e.g. continuum limit in which a single device is a vanishing fraction of capacity and the group, giving R^dev - R^tr = o(L)), or restrict the claim to "bounded envy" and present IC as numerically tested. Replace "large-market type" with the accurate description. Add the Lipschitz (or linear utility) hypothesis explicitly to Thm 2/3 and acknowledge threshold preferences as a counterexample.

### M5. Identity and flow-versus-device inconsistency undermines the "other manipulations" paragraph
Location: model.tex (class declared by a *device*; n_{g,k,t} counts *flows*; deficits attach to elastic *devices*); analysis.tex "Other manipulations" ("Utilities, deficits and the hold-down are attached to the billing identity, so splitting traffic over several flows of one subscriber changes nothing, and a subscriber is entitled to one RT bundle per class").

What is wrong: The model attaches deficits to devices and counts flows in n_{g,k}. A subscriber with two devices can run an elastic bulk device and an RT device and tunnel bulk traffic through the RT device. The elastic device's deficit then does not compensate the RT device's volume, because comparison is per-device. The sentence about the billing identity contradicts Section 3, and the theorems (per device) do not cover it. "One RT bundle per class per subscriber" is not part of the mechanism: n_{g,k} counts flows, each of which gets (a,z) in the per-slot problem. An enforcement rule (cap one RT flow per class per subscriber, or aggregate deficits at the subscriber level) is not specified, and subscriber-level deficits would change Theorem 1's proof (the elastic bonus would be shared across devices and the key step "x_i < d_i implies lambda - beta > D" would be per device versus per subscriber). Similarly, the rotation in the rounding (least-served first, newcomers with zero served fraction ahead of others) can be exploited by disconnecting and reconnecting, which resets rt_served/rt_present in the code.

What would fix it: define the unit consistently (subscriber vs device vs flow), state the cap on RT flows per subscriber/class, and either prove Thm 1 for the aggregated deficit or restrict to one device per subscriber. Discuss reconnection against the rotation.

### M6. Novelty of the theoretical contribution is modest and the positioning is partly overstated
Location: related.tex ("there is no drift-plus-penalty ... result with guarantees per agent over a finite sojourn, nor one that enforces envy constraints through virtual queues"); intro contributions 3-5.

Assessment:

- **Theorem 1.** The argument is a ten-line version of the classical fact that a DPP virtual queue is bounded by V times the maximal marginal penalty gain plus the maximal arrival (Neely 2011 worst-case delay; Huang-Neely style deterministic queue bounds; Georgiadis et al.). The paper acknowledges that it does not claim the technique. The novelty is the interpretation of the queue as per-device class envy and the observation that the tier price cancels. The cancellation is by construction (constraints linear in tier rates) and not a theorem about the mechanism. This is a valid but small contribution. It should be presented as such, in particular since the constant V w eta + rmax is the "usual" DPP constant and the bound vanishes in meaning for small L.
- **Theorem 4.** It is the standard DPP drift argument with a restricted benchmark (Neely 2010, Georgiadis et al.), with sqrt(V/T) violation because no Slater slack is assumed (standard when Slater fails; cf. the cited yu2017online and recent Slater-free work). Churn handling (drop the term of a departing device, new device starts at 0) is a routine observation. The B_0 constant is the textbook one with the worst-case bound |r-x| <= ell^max; it can be improved but is not novel.
- **Perspective/majorant.** The perspective reformulation of a*u(y/a) is standard in time-sharing/admission-control formulations; the affine majorant of min(z,d) is correct (checked) and simple, but see M2(c) on its effect on the benchmark.
- **Closest prior art, whose guarantees overlap more than the paper admits.** Karma (online strategy-proofness for credits), BoPF (bounded priority of latency-sensitive over throughput-sensitive jobs with long-term fairness), Gorokh-Banerjee-Iyer (approximate truthfulness with vanishing gains), kahana2026perpetual (deficit rule with anytime fairness bounds), and Jaramillo-Srikant (elastic and real-time under DPP with deficit counters). The paper's claimed delta, "per-device sample-path bound that survives churn within constrained stochastic optimisation with heterogeneous classes", is true but incremental. The novelty claim "to our knowledge ... nor one that enforces envy constraints through virtual queues" depends on a literature search up to Nov 2026 that I cannot reproduce. Several of the cited 2026 works (zahedi2026credit, yu2026slater, sarkar2026geometric, kahana2026perpetual, lin2026spec, elokda2026ev, abdullah2026dynamic, rezaei2026robust) should have their bibliographic details verified and, in particular, the characterisation of kahana2026perpetual, since it is the nearest deficit-queue fairness result.
- **Table 1** claims REPAY is checkmarked for "churn", "fairness" and "approximate IC" simultaneously, while the three guarantees concern different objects (M1) and IC is conditional (M4). The table should reflect "bounded class envy (rounded)", "utility bound (fluid, per-state benchmark)", "approximate IC (price-taking)".

What would fix it: rewrite the positioning to claim a clean, small, correct combination (per-device envy bound in a DPP with churn, plus negative results about window envy); drop "best envy-free policy"; cite the standard queue-bound literature in the theorem itself, and make the new content (cancellation of tier price; perspective formulation with debt terms; the explicit counterexamples) the stated contribution.

### M7. The combined trade-off (envy bound O(V) versus gap B_0/V) is never made explicit, and at the paper's own scale both are vacuous
Location: analysis.tex Thm 4 discussion and "The size of the bound" in discussion.tex.

With N_max = 75, ell^max = 30, |K| = 2 (hotspot scale), B_0 = ½·75·2·900 ≈ 6.8·10^4 (plus ½ Σ_j (e_j^max)^2, with e_j^max of the order of C ≈ 150–250 Mbit/s, which adds order 10^4–10^5). With V = 20, B_0/V ≈ 3.4·10^3+ per slot, versus U^max of a few hundred. The constraint-violation bound sqrt(2(B_0 + V U^max)/T) needs T >~ 10^5–10^6 slots (a day or more) to fall below 1 Mbit/s. The paper concedes the first point ("informative only for V far larger"). The consequence is not drawn: reducing the utility gap to U^max/10 needs V >~ 10^4, giving an envy bound B ≈ 2·10^4 Mbit, which exceeds the volume a device receives in a typical sojourn (<= 240·30 = 7200 Mbit), so envy-freeness is then vacuous. Under the paper's constants no V makes both guarantees informative simultaneously. A combined statement (e.g. the minimal L such that B/L <= epsilon_IC and B_0/V <= epsilon_U) would let the reader see this, and sharper constants could be obtained cheaply: (r-x)^2 summed over elastic devices is bounded using Sum_i x_i <= C_t and r <= rmax rather than N_max ell^max squared; the D-queue contribution to the drift can also be tightened using D <= B from Thm 1.

What would fix it: give the combined trade-off explicitly (with the numbers), tighten B_0, and say clearly that for the simulated sizes neither theorem is quantitatively informative and the theory is qualitative.

## 4. Verification notes (what I checked and found correct)

These are not concerns, but they show what has been verified, so that the authors can see where the referee spent the effort.

- **Majorant (eq. maj).** m_d(z) = sigma + s z with s = (d-rmin)/(rmax-rmin), sigma = (1-s)d passes through (d,d); it is a supporting line of min(z,d), so m >= min(z,d); the gap at the endpoints is (1-s)(d-rmin) = s(rmax-d) = (d-rmin)(rmax-d)/(rmax-rmin) (the maximum). Cases d >= rmax and d <= rmin are exact; the degenerate rmin = rmax is consistent.
- **Group term (eq. psi, eq. debt).** Sum_i D_{i,k}(a m_{d_i}(z) - x_i) = pi y + zeta a - ...; the sign of the debt terms in the objective matches (eq. slot) and (eq. dpp); tier-price terms match -Sum_j Q_j e_j (the code adds a V*varpi*price term to beta_Y, default 0, which is not in the paper's model; it cancels in Thm 1 as well).
- **Solver (Prop. 1).** Monotonicity of S(lambda), uniqueness and continuity of Lagrangian maximisers, strong duality (refined Slater for linear constraints), the video formula z* = clip(nVw/q, rmin, rmax), and the elastic formula Vw/p - 1 are right. Caveats: the marginal group in the code uses z at lambda_hi while the tolerance remark (Rem. 1) argues with lambda_lo; the bound B + delta is still valid but the text should say z*(lambda_lo) or note continuity. The O(log(lambda*/epsilon)) count omits the doubling search for an upper bracket.
- **Theorem 1.** Verified. Case x_i < d_i gives lambda - beta > D_i >= D via the right derivative; case a > 0 gives lambda - beta <= V w u_k(z)/z <= Theta by nonnegativity of the group's Lagrangian value and pi, zeta >= 0; the induction is right (r <= rmax). Rounding is covered because realised x >= fluid x and the update uses the fluid reference. WC and entrant variants are covered as argued. eta_v = 1/rmin_v because (1 + log(z/rmin))/z is decreasing. The numerical example (V=20, w=1, rmin=0.5, rmax=3 gives 40+3 = 43 Mbit, 14.3 s) is right (gaming has Theta = 100 for w = 1).
- **Remark "sharper constant".** Verified (pi >= D because device i itself contributes D_{i,k} s_{i,k} = D when d >= rmax). It needs d >= rmax in every slot.
- **Corollary 1.** Verified; the stated B uses max over (g,k).
- **Theorem 4.** One-slot drift, handling of arrivals and departures, comparison with mu in Pi(xi_t), telescoping, the expected violation bound sqrt(2(B_0+V U^max)/T), and the Borel-Cantelli step along T = m^2 are correct, given Assumption 1. (Hypotheses implicit in 1(iii): C_t bounded, so that e_j^max exists.)
- **Proposition "reverse" (appendix).** Correct as stated: u_k' >= u' on [rmin, rmax] and u_k(rmin) > rmin u'(rmin) hold for the paper's utilities (1/z >= 1/(1+z); 1 > rmin/(1+rmin)). Note (not an error) that it is a one-slot, price-taking, zero-deficit statement, that it compares utilities across two classes whose scales are unrelated (u(x) = log(1 + x) with x in Mbit/s, so the unit chosen for the "1" matters), and that it is exactly vacuous in the regime after congestion (positive deficits).
- **Proposition "baselines".** Checks out: the objective in (c) is increasing in z for z <= (1+C)/2 > C, the value at z = C is >= Vw > Vw log 2 > Vw log(1+C), and the derivative in a at a = 1 is u_v(z) - z/(1+C-z) > 0. Missing hypothesis: C <= rmax_v (so that z = C is feasible). The sentence after the proof ("gives each side the same long-run volume") is not proved; Thm 1 only gives avg x >= avg r - B/T.
- **Proposition "commit".** Correct but a statement about a proof technique on a frozen path, not about REPAY.
- **Theorem 3 counts.** K <= ceil(L/tau) and ceil(L/(2tau)) are right up to the truncation of the last interval; they need the hold-down to apply to every declaration including the first. The hold-down is not part of Algorithm 1 or of the code; it is an assumption on inputs that the access point must enforce.

## 5. Minor concerns

1. Notation clash: g is a tier index and the class "gaming" is also typeset g (K = {v, g}); `B_{g,\mathrm{g}}` in the discussion is hard to read. Rename gaming (e.g. "game") or the tier index.
2. B is used for the generic envy bound, B_{g,k}, and B_0 (Lyapunov constant). Use different letters (e.g. Delta for the Lyapunov constant).
3. "Device", "flow" and "subscriber" are used interchangeably (M5). n_{g,k,t} counts flows, e_{j,t} refers to devices.
4. The model sentence "A rule with B = 0 ... offers no protection at all" is true only for per-slot protection; B = 0 rules can still protect RT when capacity is plentiful. Rephrase to "cannot protect RT in any slot in which elastic tier-mates cannot be given at least as much volume".
5. Model: "analysis only uses that u is strictly concave, differentiable, strictly increasing" and then "we have not checked Prop. reverse for other alpha". Proposition reverse also needs u'(0) finite or a replacement for the "lambda' >= Vw u'(0)" case; state that Thm 1 and Cor. 1 are alpha-free, and Prop. reverse is not.
6. Utility scales: the comparison between u_k (dimensionless, 1 + log(z/rmin)) and u (log(1+x) in Mbit/s) is unit dependent. Results (Prop. reverse, the numerical size of B, B ∝ V w eta ∝ 1/rmin) change with the unit. State the normalisation, or rescale u by a reference rate.
7. Gaming: eta_g = 1/r_g gives B_{g,game} ≈ 100 Mbit at V = 20, w = 1, 500 times its rate; the paper notes the looseness. Since gaming has rmin = rmax, a tighter class-specific bound might follow from using the rmin/rmax structure; at least report B for each class in a table.
8. The `d_{i,t}` definition ("smaller of link rate and queued traffic") depends on service in earlier slots (queue). That conflicts with Assumption 1(i) (caps independent of past service) and with the sentence "treat the caps as exogenous, as for a device with a standing backlog". Say explicitly that Thm 1 holds for adaptive caps but Thm 4 and the price-taking theorems do not.
9. Rem. 1 (tolerance) states delta <= 1e-10 max(1, lambda_hi) is "ours", but the floating-point error of S(lambda) near flat or discontinuous parts of S is not discussed; the capacity feasibility of the allocation (up to 1e-9 tolerances in rounding `floor(na + 1e-9)`) can violate the capacity constraint by O(1e-9 z); trivial but it should be noted for exactness claims.
10. The majorant branch (d strictly in (rmin, rmax)) is never exercised by the default experiments (ell in [5, 30] >= rmax = 3, off-state d = 0, gaming rmin = rmax). The second and third cases of eq. (maj) are untested in the simulation; add a unit test or an experiment with ell < rmax devices, otherwise the reader cannot tell whether the majorant machinery works as proved.
11. The paragraph "Three features of the proof": "the tier price cancels ... this is why we wrote the business constraints per unit of rate" suggests this is a design principle, but the free-tier floor uses min(phi C_t, dem_F) with a rate-based constraint, whereas a premium commitment on admission probability would not cancel (as the discussion admits). Consider stating this as a hypothesis ("tier constraints are linear in tier rate") of Theorem 1.
12. Prop. 7 (baselines) (a): the second sentence (impostor blocks the genuine call) assumes the tier is the same and that the capacity is shared strictly by arrival order; say so.
13. Theorem 2 proof: "The deviation yields at most Sum r": clause (ii) of Def. 4 assumes this; with the actual rotation a single path yields realised volume that can exceed the expected volume by up to z_max. State Thm 2 in expectation or add the additive rmax slack.
14. Section on "reverse deviation": the proposition is about the flow "taking lambda, beta and the group offer as given", yet when declaring E the flow also loses its own RT group's (n-1) price effects. Say "price-taking" in the statement.

## 6. Mathematical errors and imprecisions, with corrected statements

1. **"The fluid reference ... is at least the average rate of the admitted flows of the group" (algorithm.tex, rounding paragraph).** False for the leftover flow when d_i < z (see M1(d)). Corrected: the fluid reference a·min(z,d_i) is at least the average usable rate of the admitted flows provided d_i >= rmax_k (or if leftover flows are served at rate z with probability frac(na)); in general the realised average usable rate is at most a·min(z,d_i) + min((na-M)z, d_i)/n.
2. **Theorem 4 applied to the experiments.** Assumption 1(ii) is violated by the Markov-modulated capacity (M3). Corrected statement: Theorem 4 holds if the capacity regime is included in xi_t (then Pi(xi) is defined per regime) or if C_t is i.i.d. given xi_t.
3. **Conclusion: "remaining within O(1/V) of the best envy-free policy in utility".** Not proved. Corrected: "within B_0/V of the best policy that satisfies, in every population state and in expectation over the slot's capacity, the business constraints and the majorant-based class-envy constraints, for the fluid allocation".
4. **Intro: "being repaid later costs them nothing".** Not true for finite sojourn because deficits are forfeited on departure. Corrected: "an elastic device is guaranteed at least the volume offered to the RT class minus B, and is repaid in full only if it stays long enough".
5. **"Strategy-proof in the large" / "large-market type" (related, intro, discussion).** The bound B/L (+ uncontrolled correction) does not depend on market size. Corrected: "approximately strategy-proof for long sojourns under price-taking, with a market-size correction not bounded here".
6. **Static misreport theorem (Thm 2)** is a bound on volume, not on utility, for arbitrary nondecreasing preferences. Corrected: add "Let the true preference phi be L_phi-Lipschitz in volume. Then the gain in utility is at most L_phi B_{g,k}"; counterexample for threshold phi given in M4(c).
7. **"Splitting traffic over several flows of one subscriber changes nothing" (analysis.tex).** Contradicts the model, in which n_{g,k} counts flows and each flow is offered (a,z). Corrected: "provided each subscriber is limited to one RT flow per class".
8. **"On the same instance REPAY ... gives each side the same long-run volume" (after Prop. 7).** Not implied by Thm 1, which only gives long-run x >= r. Corrected: "gives the elastic device at least the long-run volume of the video flow".
9. **Prop. 7 hypotheses.** Add C <= rmax_v to the hypothesis (otherwise "rate C" is infeasible for the video flow).
10. **Rem. 1.** State that the marginal group uses z*(lambda_lo) (the fraction is computed at z*(lambda_hi) in the code, which differs by continuity within delta). Corrected bound B + delta remains valid after this clarification.
11. **Proof of Prop. 1 (solver), last line.** "this is what the procedure constructs as lambda_hi ↓ lambda*" is a limit statement; at finite tolerance the allocation is feasible and delta-suboptimal, and capacity is met by construction. State suboptimality as an explicit O(delta) bound or restrict the proposition to the limit as written.
12. **Complexity claim** O(log(lambda*/epsilon)) ignores the geometric bracket search and the dependence of lambda* on beta, D (which may be large): O(log(lambda^max/epsilon)) with lambda^max <= max_i(beta_i + D_i) + V max w (max over classes of u_k/z, 1) is the right form.
