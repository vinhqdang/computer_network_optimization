# R4: Round-2 verification review

Manuscript: "Priority Now, Payback Later" (REPAY), revised version (paper/main.pdf, 34 pages, built after the last source edit; no undefined references or citations in main.log; all `\ref` labels resolve; the five new bib keys exist).
Checked against: the revised .tex, paper/tables/*.tex, and results/*.json (compare, frontier, deviation, strategic, oracle, short_stays, continuity). I re-derived several of the new numbers from the JSON files; the ones I could check are correct (paired differences, per-tier RT success, bound-size table, 0.294 of the bound, capped-priority table, collapse numbers). Where a number is wrong or mislabelled, it is listed below.

## Verdict

**Major revision again, but a much narrower one.** The authors responded honestly to most round-1 points and several new results (frontier, call continuity, short stays, oracle ablation) are reported against the authors' interest, which I credit. The theory section is now correctly scoped and I found no mathematical error in the new remarks. What keeps me from "minor revision" is that (a) the best simple baseline (capped priority) beats REPAY on average RT success, elastic rate and, at q=0.2-0.3, on the fraction of devices that gain from misreporting, yet the abstract, the contribution list and the conclusion do not say so; (b) the evidence is still synthetic, two-to-six-trace, with no real trace and no packet-level check, in a paper that is framed for Wi-Fi/Internet access; (c) the premise (declared, unverifiable class) is defended only in words, and the classifier alternative is not modelled or compared. Items (a) and part of (c) are fixable by editing. If the authors do the text fixes in section "Text-fixable" below and retarget the paper explicitly as a specialist theory-plus-simulation paper (drop "Wi-Fi" and "Tiered Internet Access" claims of realism), I would accept after minor revision. For a networking transactions venue I would still require new work (real or public trace; noisy-classifier experiment).

## Part 2. New errors, inconsistencies and overclaims introduced or left by the revision

### 2a. Cross-section story (the three items you asked about)

1. **Capped priority beating REPAY on average metrics.** Stated correctly and candidly in experiments.tex ("Trade-off curves") and discussion.tex ("a simpler rule ... protects as many RT slots as REPAY at a higher elastic rate when its cap is well chosen"). It is absent from the abstract, from the intro contributions and "what the approach does not do" paragraph, and from the conclusion. The abstract's sentence "strict priority serves all of them and the same scheduler without deficits 92%, but both reward misreporting" suggests that every rule serving more RT slots than REPAY is manipulable. The paper's own Table cap contradicts that: at q=0.2 and 0.3 capped priority serves 85.5%/93.2% of RT slots with 2.03/1.80 Mbit/s, and misreporting loses on median (-55%, -20%) with 1.7% and 25% of devices gaining, against 51% under REPAY. Inconsistent by omission. Also, the capped-priority incentive columns come from 60 devices on 2 traces whereas REPAY's 51% comes from 180 devices on 6; the comparison made in the discussion ("with q=0.2 or 0.3 a misreporting device loses on average") is not like-for-like. The "cap must be set for the share of real-time traffic ... which changes during the day" argument is asserted; no experiment varies the class mix for either rule.
2. **Half of devices gaining.** Consistent across abstract, intro, experiments (51%, and 83% for short stays), discussion. Two gaps: the abstract's "none by more than a third of the bound" is for stays >= 60 slots and for REPAY only (No-debt reaches 0.334, REPAY-E 0.305); and the heterogeneity (free tier 71% gain, median +1.2%; premium and PAYG lose, driven partly by the 3 Mbit/s cap artefact) is in experiments.tex only. The abstract does not mention that the short-stay figure is 83%.
3. **Scope of each theorem.** Consistent and now correct in analysis.tex and algorithm.tex (Thm 1 rounded; Thms 2-3 price-taking, volume, in expectation; Thm 4 fluid, per-state benchmark, i.i.d. capacity given state). Abstract is the only place where scopes are compressed: Thm 4's "per population state" and "capacity conditionally independent given the state" are missing; Thm 3 reads like an IC guarantee. Conclusion is correct for Thm 4 and silent on Thms 2-3 and on all limits.

### 2b. Statements in the new paragraphs that are wrong or overclaimed

N1. **related.tex, L4S**: "which removes the incentive to misdeclare because the low-delay queue carries no throughput advantage". L4S is designed to avoid a throughput advantage, but RFC 9330/9332 themselves require queue protection against flows that misuse the low-latency queue. Say "is designed to remove".
N2. **related.tex, FQ-CoDel/CAKE**: "on the time scale of our model they behave like the class-blind baseline PF". No FQ/DRR baseline was run; flow queueing is per flow (or per host), PF here is per device with weights. Plausible but untested; hedge ("we expect").
N3. **experiments.tex, "When many misreport"**: "Under REPAY a misreporting device receives less than a truthful one (1.06 against 1.24 Mbit/s at rho=0.25), so the truthful profile rho=0 is stable." Verified in strategic.json, but the control is missing: the class-blind PF has a larger deficit for misreporters (-0.26 Mbit/s vs -0.18 for REPAY), because the declared-video bundle is capped at 3 Mbit/s for sampled devices (the paper says this itself in the unilateral-deviation paragraph). Relative to the control, misreporting under REPAY is slightly profitable (+0.08 Mbit/s), consistent with the 51% gainers and the pooled -11.2% vs -15.4% for PF. "Stable" is not supported; it should be an observation with the control.
N4. **experiments.tex, same paragraph**: "elastic rate of 1.28 Mbit/s" for REPAY at rho=0 and "0.97" for Priority at rho=1 use volume per slot of stay (idle periods included; 1.75 x 50/70 is about 1.25), while Section 6.3 reports "elastic rate" 1.75 as the mean while active. Same word, two metrics, unexplained. Also, "a quarter of the elastic rate" is wrong: 0.97 is 24% lower than 1.28, not a quarter of it.
N5. **experiments.tex, call continuity**: "give the same figures to within 0.02". Table continuity: 95th-percentile outage 14.4 s vs 14.2/14.6 s, outage-in-runs share 65.4 vs 65.7/65.6; only the interruption rate and mean length are within 0.02. Say "within 0.2 s at the 95th percentile and 0.3 points elsewhere". Variant names (REPAY-sticky, REPAY-block) in the table are not defined in the text.
N6. **algorithm.tex**: "an effect we do not bound and instead measure in Section exp-tiers": not measured (see R1-M1).
N7. **experiments.tex, short stays**: "reduces the incentive of short-lived devices by an order of magnitude without removing it". Median gain 10% vs 59% (DPP) is a factor of 6, vs about 140% (Priority/WFQ) a factor of 14; the share of devices gaining (83%) is not reduced relative to DPP (87.5%). Say "by a factor of six to fourteen in median gain".
N8. **analysis.tex, Static misreport**: Thm 2 is stated with constant B; the text then says the bound is "on the long-run share ... a single path can deliver up to rmax_k more, which adds rmax_k to the constant". B already contains rmax_k; the sentence should say the theorem is in expectation (Def. 4(ii)) and a realised path can exceed it by rmax_k.
N9. **analysis.tex, Section 4.3**: "B_0 is of order (N_max ell_max)^2" is wrong (see R1-M7 row).
N10. **analysis.tex**: "At V=20 the envy bound is meaningful (Table bsize)". It is a sixth to a quarter of a median stay's volume and nearly all of a 60-slot stay; "meaningful" should be quantified.
N11. **analysis.tex, last paragraph**: "at no long-run cost to elastic users" (see R1-M4f). Also intro.tex "telling the truth becomes (approximately) the best policy" and "different bundles that each prefers to the other's": contradicted by 51% (83% short stays) gainers; keep only with "for price-taking devices and long stays".
N12. **experiments.tex, reverse deviation**: "Every one of them loses utility ... in line with Proposition reverse". The proposition is one-slot, zero-deficit, price-taking; the experiment is not an instance of it. Say "consistent with".
N13. **experiments.tex, oracle ablation**: "Forfeiture is thus what makes slot-by-slot switching unprofitable". With forfeiture removed the mean gain is positive only at tau=1 (+0.011) and negative at tau=5, 20 (-0.012, -0.024) although 60% of devices gain; two traces, 40 devices. "Is what makes it unprofitable for most devices" is supportable; "it should be kept" ignores the cost to a legitimate user who alternates call and download (deficit lost). State that cost.

Verified and fine: the Remark "sharper constant" (n/(n+1) factor correct), the price-taking correction B + (R^dev - R^tr) (immediate from Cor. 1), the K <= ceil(L/(2 tau)) count (checked algebraically), Prop. commit counterexample, Prop. baselines (c), the Prop. reverse proof, the Theorem 4 proof (drift, churn, Borel-Cantelli), Table bsize, deviation and short-stay and oracle numbers, abstract numbers.

### Needs new work

W1. Real or public trace (association/sojourn and a capacity trace) and at least one packet-level or tc/hostapd enforcement check; without it the Wi-Fi framing and absolute numbers are not supportable. (R2-M2/M6, R3-4.)
W2. Noisy-classifier experiment and a flow-queueing (FQ/DRR) or classifier baseline, to substantiate the premise and the new related-work paragraph (R2-M1, R3-1).
W3. Class-aware credit baseline (Karma/BoPF-style with an RT reference) and a run of Jaramillo-Srikant, so that the contribution over prior credit schemes is isolated empirically (R2-M3, R3-5).
W4. Mix/load/sojourn/n sweeps, including class mix for capped priority vs REPAY (to test the claim that the cap is mix-dependent), and gaming declarations in the deviation study (R2-M6, M5).
W5. Rounding: either randomised rounding with a proof that E[realised] = fluid, or a measurement of the realised-minus-fluid tier rate (R1-M1c); this decides whether Theorem 4 says anything about the algorithm that is simulated.
W6. A benchmark that supports the time-shifting narrative (long-run per-device envy with Slater slack), or a clean statement that Theorem 4 is qualitative; a bound or large-n scaling experiment on the price-taking correction (R1-M2, M4a).
W7. Stress test of Theorem 1 with an adversarial search over capacity/arrival paths rather than hand-picked square waves; more than two traces for the oracle/switching attacks; warm-up discarded in the strategic runs.
W8. Trim: move variants, thin-market, runtime and reverse-deviation material to an appendix (the paper is now 34 pages).
